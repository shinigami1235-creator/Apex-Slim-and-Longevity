Longevity / HBOT

HBOT

In hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) you breathe pure oxygen inside a chamber at 2 to 3 times normal air pressure. Hospitals use it to treat divers with decompression sickness and to help wounds that won't heal.

Almost all the oxygen in your blood is carried by your red blood cells, and they're already close to full. The pressure in the chamber pushes extra oxygen into the plasma, the liquid part of your blood. At about 2.4 times normal pressure the plasma carries about 5 to 6 mL of oxygen in every 100 mL of blood, compared with about 0.3 mL at normal pressure.

How pressure adds oxygen

Your red blood cells are about 97 percent full of oxygen when you breathe normal air. The higher the pressure, the more extra oxygen dissolves into the plasma.

Hospitals use HBOT on difficult wounds since oxygen in the plasma can reach places that red blood cells have trouble getting to, like swollen tissue and the edges of a wound.

What it's approved for

The US FDA has cleared hyperbaric chambers for a list of conditions. These include decompression sickness, air bubbles in the blood, carbon monoxide poisoning, burns, severe infections, slow-healing diabetic wounds, radiation injury and severe anaemia when a blood transfusion isn't possible. For wounds, hospitals give sessions of 1.5 to 2 hours every day for 20 to 40 days.

HBOT for wellness and recovery

At Apex Slim we offer HBOT as a 60-minute session for people who sleep badly, feel worn out from work, train hard or are recovering from surgery, and for people who want to slow the effects of ageing. None of these is on the FDA list. The main study on this, done in Israel, gave 35 adults aged 64 and over 60 sessions at 2 times normal pressure over 3 months.

Telomeres are the caps on the ends of your chromosomes, and they get shorter as you age. In the study they got more than 20 percent longer in several types of immune cell, and worn-out helper T cells went down by 37 percent.

The study was small and had no comparison group. It also only measured blood cells, so we still don't know what HBOT does to ageing in the rest of the body. The FDA warns against centres that say HBOT can cure conditions it isn't cleared for.

Your session

  1. Change into cotton clothes. Phones, lighters, hand warmers, lotion and hair oil have to stay outside since pure oxygen under pressure is a fire risk.
  2. The door closes and the pressure goes up over a few minutes. Your ears will feel full like when a plane is landing. Swallow, yawn, or pinch your nose and blow gently to clear them.
  3. You breathe pure oxygen through a mask or hood for the rest of the session. You can rest or sleep.
  4. The pressure goes down slowly at the end and your ears may pop again. You can go back to your day straight after.

Side effects

  • ear pain or pressure from the pressure change, which is the most common problem
  • sinus pain, more likely if you have a cold
  • blurry distance vision that builds up over many sessions and goes back to normal after you finish
  • feeling closed in if you don't like small spaces
  • a seizure caused by the oxygen, which is rare at about 1 to 4 in every 10,000 treatments

You can't have HBOT if you have a collapsed lung that hasn't been treated. The doctor will also ask about any recent ear or chest surgery and the medicines you take before your first session. If you have a bad cold or a blocked nose, you'll need to wait until it clears.

Questions

Does it hurt?

Your ears can hurt for a moment as the pressure goes up if they don't clear, and the staff can slow the pressure down for you. After that it's like sitting in a quiet room.

How many sessions do I need?

The doctor decides based on your goal. The study on ageing used 60 sessions over 3 months.

Is it the same as breathing oxygen from a tank?

No. At normal pressure, oxygen from a tank only tops up red blood cells that are already nearly full. You need the pressure in the chamber to get the extra oxygen into your plasma.

Can I bring my phone?

No. Phones and other electronics have to stay outside since a spark is dangerous in pure oxygen.

I don't like small spaces. Can I still do it?

Tell the doctor at your consult. The staff stay in contact with you the whole time and can stop the session if you need to come out.
