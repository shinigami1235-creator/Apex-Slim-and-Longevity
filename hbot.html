Longevity / HBOT

HBOT

The air you breathe is 21 percent oxygen, and nearly all of it rides on your red blood cells, which are already close to full. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) gets more oxygen into your blood by dissolving it into the plasma, the liquid part of the blood.

You breathe pure oxygen inside a chamber pressurised to 2 to 3 times normal air pressure, the same treatment hospitals use for divers with decompression sickness. At about 2.4 times normal pressure, the oxygen dissolved in your plasma rises from about 0.3 mL to about 5 to 6 mL in every 100 mL of blood.

Why the pressure matters

Red blood cells are about 97 percent full of oxygen when you breathe normal air, so breathing more oxygen at normal pressure adds very little. Pressure gets around this by pushing oxygen into the plasma. The harder a gas is pressed against a liquid, the more of it dissolves, which is how a soft drink holds its fizz until the cap comes off.

Oxygen carried in the plasma reaches places red blood cells struggle to reach, such as swollen tissue and the edges of a wound, and hospitals use HBOT to help difficult wounds heal for that reason.

What it is approved for

The US FDA has cleared hyperbaric chambers for a set list of conditions, including decompression sickness, air bubbles in the blood, carbon monoxide poisoning, burns, severe infections, slow-healing diabetic wounds, radiation injury and severe anaemia when a transfusion is not possible. For wound healing, hospitals give daily sessions of 1.5 to 2 hours for 20 to 40 days.

HBOT for wellness and recovery

At Apex Slim, HBOT is offered as a wellness and recovery session, outside that medical list. The best-known study in this area comes from Israel, where 35 adults aged 64 and over had 60 sessions at 2 times normal pressure over 3 months. Telomeres, the caps on the ends of chromosomes that shorten with age, grew more than 20 percent longer in several types of immune cell, and worn-out helper T cells fell by 37 percent.

The study was small, had no comparison group and measured blood cells only, so it shows a change in those cells while the effect on ageing in the body is still unknown. The FDA also warns against centres that promise HBOT will cure conditions it has not been cleared for, and the doctor walks you through the research at your consult.

Your session

  1. Change into cotton clothing. Phones, lighters, hand warmers, lotions and hair oil stay outside since pure oxygen under pressure is a fire risk.
  2. The chamber door closes and the pressure rises over several minutes. Your ears fill up the way they do when a plane lands, so swallow, yawn or pinch your nose and blow gently to clear them.
  3. You breathe pure oxygen through a mask or hood for the session, and you can rest or sleep.
  4. The pressure comes down slowly at the end and your ears may pop again. You can go back to your day straight after.

Side effects

  • Ear pain or pressure from the pressure change, the most common problem
  • Sinus pain, more likely if you have a cold
  • Short-sightedness that builds over many sessions and goes back to normal after the course ends
  • Feeling closed in, for people who dislike small spaces
  • A seizure caused by the oxygen, which is rare at about 1 to 4 in every 10,000 treatments

A collapsed lung that has not been treated rules HBOT out. The doctor also asks about recent ear or chest surgery and your medicines before your first session, and a bad cold or blocked nose means waiting until it clears.

Questions

Does it hurt?

Your ears can hurt for a moment as the pressure rises if they do not clear, and the staff can slow the pressure change for you. The rest of the session feels like sitting in a quiet room.

How many sessions will I need?

The doctor sets the number from your goal at the consult. The study on ageing used 60 sessions over 3 months.

Is it the same as breathing oxygen from a tank?

No. Oxygen from a tank at normal pressure only tops up red blood cells that are already nearly full. The pressure in the chamber is what dissolves the extra oxygen into your plasma.

Can I bring my phone?

No. Phones and other electronics stay outside since a spark is dangerous in pure oxygen.

I don't like small spaces. Can I still do it?

Tell the doctor at your consult. The staff stay in contact with you the whole time and the session can be stopped if you need to come out.
